Two useful skills, one calm chapter. Muting is the "shh" switch you use often. Factory reset is the "clean slate" move you use rarely — when selling a device, giving it away, or when something has gone confusingly wrong and a fresh start would untangle it faster than troubleshooting.

Part 1: Muting — the "shh" switch

Smart speakers

  • Find the physical mute — a button, a slider, or a switch on the body of the device. Every major speaker has one; it's usually on the top or the back near the power socket.
  • When muted, most speakers light up unmistakably: a red ring, an orange dot, or a crosshatch. Learn what "muted" looks like on your model.
  • Physical mute cuts the microphone in hardware. There is no app setting and no cloud server that can overrule it. This is why the button beats software privacy modes.
  • Muting stops the hearing; it does not stop any recordings already saved in your account's voice-history page (which is a settings-page matter, see the Privacy Settings Table).

Cameras

  • Cameras don't have a mute-the-lens button in most cases, but the app usually offers a "privacy mode" — a software shutter that stops recording and often stops motion alerts. If you own a classic baby monitor, its power/panic switch usually kills both lens and mic.
  • Honest habit: unplug the camera during private times ("bathtime, dance-parties, trustworthy naps"). A camera that isn't powered can't save a thing, and the habit takes two seconds.
  • If you use an indoor camera rarely, prefer one with a physical shutter or "privacy mode" toggle on the device itself.

Thermostats and plugs

Nothing to mute — no ears. Their privacy knob is in settings (occupancy sensing, sharing behaviors), not on the body.

Part 2: Factory reset — the clean-slate move

Factory reset erases the device's own memory: Wi-Fi credentials, paired account links, saved schedules, and any on-device recordings it keeps. What it does not do: delete things you saved to a cloud account (those are deleted through the account, separately) or on an SD card unless reset includes wiping it (many cameras wipe their own card; some don't — check).

Before a reset — the order that matters:

  1. In the maker's app, remove the device from your account (sometimes "Delete device," "Unpair," "Deregister"). This is the step people skip; do it first or the app will nag you forever.
  2. Delete any clips/schedules you care to keep — export or download before the wipe.
  3. Then reset the device.
  4. For cameras with an SD card, physically remove the card unless the reset explicitly wipes it; you can wipe it from a laptop too if you want extra certainty.

Generic reset steps (all these devices follow one or two patterns):

  • Button-hold pattern. There is a small hole or button labeled "Reset" (you may need a straightened paperclip for the hole kind). Press and hold for anywhere from around 5 to 15 seconds — watch for a color change or a chime, which is the device confirming the wipe. Common with speakers and plugs.
  • Menu reset. In the companion app: device settings → "Factory reset" / "Remove device and reset." Some devices do both the unpair and the wipe from the app in one step. Common with cameras and thermostats.
  • Unplug-hold-unplug dance. A few wall-ish devices (some thermostats and plugs) use a sequence: hold a button while restoring power, or toggle power 5 times. The device's manual (a quick search for the model name and "factory reset" finds it) states the rhythm precisely.
  • If the reset button is not visible, check the manual's "reset" page — makers put it there precisely because owners ask.

After a reset, the device comes up in "setup mode" — ready to be paired fresh by whoever has it next, including you or its new owner. For anything you're selling, also delete any clips and revoke home-sharing in your account's "Family" or "Members" pages, which lives beside the device list.

One calm habit worth adopting

"Reset on exit." Whenever a smart device leaves your house — sold, gifted, recycled — it goes through one factory reset the day before. Add it to your moving-out or giving-away mental checklist next to "take charger." It's free, it's four minutes, and it means a stranger can never be holding your Wi-Fi password, your house's comings-and-goings, or your clean-fitting necessarily-private clips.
