Smart Home Comfort Guide privacy-first, in plain English

Every word in this manual that sounds like jargon, explained the way a friend would. Alphabetical, short, and calm.

  • 2FA / two-factor authentication — A second lock on your account: after your password, a code from your phone or an app. Boring, effective, free. Turn it on.
  • Account unlinking — Removing a device or service from your account so it no longer reports to you or the company. Do it before factory-resetting hardware, as a general habit.
  • App firmware / firmware — The built-in software inside a device — the instructions it runs. Updates to firmware occasionally add features, close security gaps, or break a favorite feature; update when the app suggests but not on vacation days.
  • Automation — A rule the system runs without a human: "at 11 pm, turn off the porch light." Automations are small; a good one is invisible.
  • C-wire — The "common" wire in thermostat wiring that provides constant power. Some smart thermostats want one; some work without. The photo you take before installing tells you which camp you're in.
  • Cloud — Somebody else's computer. When a device "sends to the cloud," data leaves your house and is stored and processed on company servers.
  • Cloud-tethered device — A gadget that depends on the maker's servers to do its job. Easiest to set up; stops working fully when either the internet or the company loses interest.
  • Detection zone — A drawn box in a camera app that says "count motion here, not over there." Removes most street/shadow alerts.
  • Do Not Disturb — Scheduled silence. Different device types have versions: no voice announcements overnight (speaker), no notifications overnight (camera).
  • Energy metering — A plug's ability to report how much power passes through it. Useful for "is the dehumidifier actually running?"
  • Factory reset — Wiping a device's own memory back to how it left the factory. Removes saved Wi-Fi, schedules, and pairings. Do it before giving a device away.
  • Geofencing — Using your phone's location to trigger things ("heat the house as I approach"). Handy, but makes location part of the system. A schedule gets you most of the comfort.
  • Guest network — A second, quarantined Wi-Fi lane for visitors. Guests get internet; they don't get your devices. See the plain-English guide in Guest Networks.
  • Home Assistant — Free, open-source software that runs on a small computer in your house and acts as the brain of a local-first smart home. The community's favorite.
  • HomeKit — Apple's home-control system. Its architecture keeps most control and history local to your home hub — a privacy-friendly default baked into its design.
  • Hot word / wake word — The phrase that rouses a speaker's attention ("Hey so-and-so"). The speaker listens continuously for that phrase only; until it's heard, most speakers don't record or send anything.
  • Hub — A small box that bridges between your devices (which often speak radio protocols) and your network (which speak Wi-Fi). A hub keeps rules and schedules inside your house; its app is your remote.
  • IoT — "Internet of Things." The umbrella term for non-computer gadgets that connect to networks: plugs, thermostats, cameras, doorbells.
  • Local control — Devices doing their thinking inside your house — on the plug, on a hub, in Home Assistant — instead of on a company's server. The quiet backbone of this manual.
  • Local schedule — A schedule stored on the device or hub itself rather than in a cloud. Local schedules survive internet outages.
  • Standards (Matter, Thread, Zigbee, Z-Wave) — Shared languages devices use so they can talk to hubs from many brands. Matter and Thread are the newest pair; Zigbee and Z-Wave are the long-respected older ones. Seeing "Zigbee/Thread/Matter" on a box is a good local-friendly sign.
  • Micro-SD card — A fingernail-sized memory card. Many cameras accept one, giving you video history that lives in the camera instead of a cloud.
  • Mute button — A physical switch that disconnects a speaker's microphone (usually lighting a red indicator). Hardware mute beats software settings: no server can unmute it.
  • NVR — "Network video recorder." A small box that records camera footage locally, instead of in a cloud service. The classic privacy choice for multi-camera homes.
  • Occupancy sensing — A thermostat's guess, from temperature changes or sensors, about whether someone's home. Great in some houses, silly in very predictable ones.
  • On-device processing — The device answers requests using its own brain rather than sending audio to servers. Newer speakers do more of this for wake-word and basic commands; it's a privacy friend.
  • Pairing — The first-time handshake between a device and its app/hub. Usually involves a button press and a wait bar.
  • Privacy mode — A camera's software shutter: recording and alerts pause without a full unplug.
  • Voice server / assistant back end — The internet service that turns your speaker's audio into meaning when on-device processing isn't used. Not sinister — just a mechanism to be aware of.
  • Rate limit — A rule like "no more than one alert per two minutes." Prevents alert storms.
  • Routine — A named sequence ("Good night") that runs multiple actions at once, triggered by one phrase, schedule, or tap. The highest-comfort feature in the whole guide.
  • Router — The box your internet provider (or you, if you bought one) gave the house; it hands out Wi-Fi and stores all the settings this guide has you adjust. You visit it in settings rarely but importantly.
  • SD slot — The little opening in gadgets that accepts a memory card.
  • Skill / Action — An app-like add-on for a voice assistant ("weather skill," "pizza-ordering skill"). Fewer installed, less data exposure.
  • Smart plug — An adapter that makes whatever's plugged into it switchable by app/schedule/voice. The most forgiving entry point in home automation.
  • Thread / Matter — The modern smart-home pair: Thread is the radio (fast, local, mesh), Matter is the language (cross-brand). Together, they're the path most devices are following toward local control.
  • Voice history — The account page where saved voice clips live. Visit once; make a deliberate saving/auto-delete choice; sleep well.
  • Wake sound — A feature on some newer speakers that listens for a chosen sound (a door chime, a dog bark) as a trigger. Check settings if it surprises you.
  • Z-Wave / Zigbee — Older radio protocols that pair with local hubs. They still work great; gear is easy to find and often cheaper than the latest thing.

Acknowledgment of style

If a definition above used humor, it was on purpose: definitions stick better when they don't talk down to you.
