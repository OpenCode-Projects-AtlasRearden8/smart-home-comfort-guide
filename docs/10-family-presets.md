# Family Presets

This chapter is designed to be **printed and handed to people**, not read
cover to cover. Each card below is a short, complete set of instructions for
one situation — for the less-techy member of the household, the visiting
guest, or the babysitter. Print, cut, laminate (or fold into a wallet), and
post where they'll be needed.

Cards have a calm, matter-of-fact tone on purpose. Nothing here needs an
explanation; each card stands alone.

---

## Card 1 — Guest Wi-Fi (post by the front door)

> **Welcome! Guest Wi-Fi**
>
> - Network: `SureHills-Guest`
> - Password: `change-me-quarterly`
>
> It gives you internet, but doesn't touch our family network — that's a
> good thing, it keeps both of us safe.
>
> Password changes sometimes; check the date on this card.

## Card 2 — Muting the speaker (post near the speaker)

> **When you want quiet:**
>
> 1. Press the small button with the mic-with-a-line icon on top.
> 2. The light will turn red. Red means *"not listening."*
> 3. Press again to turn it back. Red off = back on.
>
> You can also just say "mute" — the ring goes red the same way.

## Card 3 — Kitchen timer hands (leave in the kitchen drawer)

> **Kitchen timer, hands-full method:**
>
> Say: **"Set a timer for 12 minutes."**
> (Any length works.)
>
> It will make a chime you can't miss. Say **"stop"** to shush it.
>
> If it doesn't hear: press the big top button, then ask again.

## Card 4 — The lamp helper (post by the lamp with the smart plug)

> **The hallway lamp:**
>
> - Say: **"Turn on the hallway lamp"** / **"Turn off the hallway lamp."**
> - Or tap its button in the app on the living room tablet.
> - It turns itself off at 11:30 pm. Automation is the night watchman.
> - If the internet is out: the lamp's *own* wall switch still works, as
>   always.

## Card 5 — Before you get on a plane: the away pass (in the hall junk drawer)

> **Leaving-town checklist (10 minutes):**
>
> 1. Thermostat: set away temperature in the app (a button named "Eco" or
>    "Away").
> 2. All lamps: turn off; leave the two "Away mode" plugs running.
> 3. Cameras: set quiet hours off — we *want* alerts while away.
> 4. Speaker: it can be left on mute; no one visits your house while you're
>    gone.
> 5. Water off? Stove off? (Yes, do the human checks too.)

## Card 6 — "Someone's coming over" (post inside the living room cabinet)

> **Company in 5? Quick privacy pass:**
>
> 1. Speaker: press the mic-mute (light goes red).
> 2. Indoor camera: open the app, tap **Privacy Mode** (a small eye icon).
>    Or just unplug it — the honest switch.
> 3. Thermostat: nothing to do. It has no ears.
> 4. Guest Wi-Fi is on the front-door card; hand it out.

## Card 7 — Reset-the-fuse version (in the device box)

> **Something's being weird? Try in order:**
>
> 1. **Unplug it, count to ten, plug it back in.** This fixes most things.
> 2. Say "please reboot" in the app (some devices support it).
> 3. **Do NOT factory reset** unless the paper in this box for your exact
>    device says to — a reset erases settings and takes an hour to redo.
> 4. If unplug-and-ten didn't work and it's still misbehaving: leave it
>    alone and put a sticky note on it for the household tech person.

## Card 8 — For the room that mostly matters: sleep

> **The bedroom speaker at night:**
>
> 1. Say **"Good night"** (this may trigger the household's good-night
>    routine; ask the tech person what it does).
> 2. Or press the mute button on top — red ring = silence.
> 3. "Stop" or "snooze" handles alarms in the morning.

---

## Notes for the household tech person (everyone else can stop reading)

- Write each card against *your actual* setup — the sample phrases above
  name example devices and times on purpose, so you swap in real ones.
- Keep one master copy of all cards (a doc or paper) so reprints are
  painless after passwords change.
- Cards 1, 2, 4, and 8 are the high-frequency ones; print doubles and put
  them where each behavior happens, not in a drawer nobody opens.
- If your companion app supports **routines** (sequences that run from one
  phrase), building one "Good night" routine per card 8 is the single
  highest-comfort, lowest-risk automation in the whole house.
