# Smart Speakers

The speaker is usually the first smart device people bring home, and it is
the right beginner device: it needs no wiring, it's used every day, and it
teaches you the app-and-account pattern that every other device will follow.

## What a speaker is for (and what it's honestly not)

It's good at: hands-full timers, kitchen unit conversions, playing music or a
podcast, reading a recipe step aloud, calling out to another room, adding to
the shared shopping list. What it's *not* great for yet: long conversations,
anything requiring deep thought, and being a privacy-free zone — it has ears,
by design.

## Placing it well

- Put it where you'll use it most — kitchen counters and living rooms win.
- Avoid placing it next to a TV (it hears the TV constantly) or a baby
  monitor.
- If you want it heard less often, hallway or bedroom corner beats desk
  neighbor.

## First ten minutes, once

1. Plug it in, follow the companion app, sign in with a **household account**
   that's not your most important personal email if you'd rather start clean.
2. Complete the microphone-permission and voice-history pages *deliberately* —
   don't skip past them. The defaults are usually "yes, keep recordings";
   you're allowed to choose "no."
3. Test the mute. Learn what "muted" looks like on your model (a red ring, an
   orange light, a crosshatch). Every person in the house should know it.

## Daily-life comfort settings

- **Voice history off** (or auto-delete after a short period): in the app
  under something like "Voice History" / "Data & Privacy."
- **Wake word you like**: pick the one that's easiest to say and least
  likely to fire by accident from your TV or radio.
- **Do Not Disturb** for hours when you don't want announcements or ring-in
  pings.
- **Explicit language filter** and **volume cap** if kids use it.
- **Remove third-party extras**: in the app there's a list of linked
  "skills" or "actions." Fewer links, fewer data paths. Keep the two or three
  you actually use.

## When someone is over, or the household wants quiet

- The **hardware mute** cuts the microphone completely. Say it aloud the
  first few times: "the ring is red = it's deaf." Presets for exactly this are
  in [Family Presets](10-family-presets.md).
- Unplugging always works, and it's honest. Plugs' schedules (next chapter)
  are, in fairness, doing a better job than unplugging ever did.

## Using it for good (ideas)

- "Set a 12-minute timer" while cooking pasta.
- "Add coffee and oat milk to the shopping list."
- "Play soft jazz in the kitchen" when the day needs softening.
- "Tell the office light to turn off" — once a plug or bulb is connected.

That last one is the bridge to the next chapter: the speaker is the *voice*;
the humble plug is usually the *hands*.
