# Getting Started

Welcome. This manual exists to make one specific thing easier: enjoying the
comfort of a few well-chosen smart devices in your home, without feeling
rushed, confused, or talked into things.

There is no test at the end. There is no rush. You can stop reading at any
point and come back — the room-by-room checklist near the end is designed to
be picked up, put down, and worked through at whatever pace suits your week.

## The one paragraph version

A smart home is just a few tools that listen, switch, warm, or watch, in
service of small everyday comforts: a light that turns itself off, a house
that is warm when you wake up, a speaker that sets a timer while your hands
are full. Each of those tools has senses and habits — some hear you when
invited, some send information about your home to a company's servers. This
manual walks through each device type honestly, shows you how to check the
knobs and switches each one offers, and favors devices that can do their job
*without* the internet. That last idea — **local control first** — is the
quiet backbone of this whole guide.

## What this guide is

- A beginner manual for four common device types: **smart speakers, smart
  plugs, smart thermostats, and home cameras**.
- A privacy-first tour: what each device hears, what it sends, and which
  settings you should look at once and then forget about.
- A practical walkthrough of a **guest network**, in plain English.
- A set of print-friendly **presets and checklists**, made for less-techy
  family members.

## What this guide is not

- It is **not** a shopping list. We describe things by *category* and *trait*
  (for example: "a plug that can be scheduled locally from an app, without a
  cloud account"), not by hype or price. Nothing here earns a commission.
- It is **not** a security audit. It is a calm, first-timer's guide.
- It is **not** medical, legal, or financial advice. It is home comfort
  advice, which is a much gentler genre.

## How to read it

1. Start with [What Devices Hear and Send](02-what-devices-hear-and-send.md) —
   ten minutes there makes every later chapter click.
2. Skim [Local Control First](07-local-control-first.md) so the phrase means
   something when it reappears.
3. Then read the chapters for any devices you already own, or plan to get.
4. When you're ready to take action, work through the
   [Room-by-Room Checklist](11-room-by-room-checklist.md) with the
   [Privacy Settings Table](12-privacy-settings-table.md) open on a phone or
   printed next to you.
5. Hand the [Family Presets](10-family-presets.md) cards to whoever in the
   household prefers "just tell me what to press."

If a word sounds like jargon, the [Glossary](13-glossary.md) has it in
everyday language.

## A calm word before we start

Nearly all of the worrying headlines about smart devices fall into a small
number of buckets, and each bucket has a plain-English fix: separate the
guests from the family Wi-Fi, choose devices that work locally when you can,
amplify the mute button, and know how to reset a thing when you're done with
it. That's genuinely most of it. The rest of this guide just expands those
steps, slowly enough that you can follow along with the device in your hand.
